🧾 Lab SOP (BIS)

Test: Fe(M) – Metallic Iron (Sponge Iron)

🔬 Reagents Preparation:
1. 0.1 N Potassium Dichromate:
4.903 g K₂Cr₂O₇ → make 1 litre solution

2. Acid Mixture:
30 ml H₂SO₄ + 30 ml H₃PO₄ + 40 ml distilled water

3. Indicator:
0.2 g Barium Diphenylamine Sulphonate in 100 ml water

4. HgCl₂:
Use directly (solid)

⚙️ Procedure:
1. Take 0.2 g sample
2. Add 2 g HgCl₂
3. Add 100 ml distilled water
4. Heat till ~50 ml
5. Cool & filter (Whatman 41)
6. Add 20 ml acid mixture
7. Add indicator
8. Titrate with 0.1 N K₂Cr₂O₇ till colour changes (clear → greenish)

🧮 Calculation:
Fe(M)% = (Reading × 0.05585 × 100) / W

💡 Suggestions:
👉 <80% = Low metallization
👉 80–85% = Normal
👉 >85% = Good
Test: FeT – Total Iron (Sponge Iron)

🔬 Reagents Preparation:
1. 0.1 N Potassium Dichromate (K₂Cr₂O₇):
4.903 g dissolve karke 1 litre solution banaayein

2. Stannous Chloride (SnCl₂ Solution):
10 g SnCl₂ + 25 ml conc. HCl
Light heating karke dissolve karein, then make up to 100 ml
👉 Fresh prepare daily

3. Mercuric Chloride (HgCl₂):
5% solution ya solid use karein

4. Acid Mixture:
30 ml H₂SO₄ + 30 ml H₃PO₄ + 40 ml distilled water

5. Indicator:
Barium Diphenylamine Sulphonate

⚙️ Procedure:
1. Take 0.5 g finely powdered sample
2. Add HCl and dissolve completely (heat if required)
3. Add SnCl₂ dropwise till solution becomes colourless (Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺)
4. Add HgCl₂ (white ppt formation confirms excess SnCl₂ removed)
5. Add 20 ml acid mixture
6. Add 2–3 drops indicator
7. Titrate with 0.1 N K₂Cr₂O₇
8. End point: colour changes (green → violet/blue)

🧮 Calculation:
FeT % = (Burette Reading × N × 0.5585) / Sample Weight (g)

For 0.1 N:
FeT % = (Reading × 0.05585 × 100) / W

📊 Typical Range:
• 88–92% → Normal Sponge
• >92% → High grade

💡 Suggestions:
👉 Low FeT → High gangue (SiO₂ / Al₂O₃)
👉 High FeT → Better furnace yield
👉 Compare with Fe(M) to calculate FeO

⚠️ Precautions:
👉 SnCl₂ fresh use karo (old solution गलत result देगा)
👉 Excess SnCl₂ avoid karo
👉 HgCl₂ properly add karo (white ppt must)
👉 Proper dissolution जरूरी है
👉 End point carefully observe karo
FeO (Indirect Calculation)

FeO % = (FeT − Fe(M)) × 1.286

💡 Use:
👉 Quick plant calculation
👉 Lab time saving method
👉 FeT & Fe(M) required

⚠️ Note:
👉 Accuracy depends on FeT & Fe(M)
👉 Direct FeO test se compare karna better hai
Test: FeO – Universal Method (KMnO₄)

🔬 Reagents:
1. 0.1 N KMnO₄:
3.16 g KMnO₄ → 1 litre solution
(Standardize with oxalic acid)

2. Dilute H₂SO₄ (1:1)

3. Distilled Water

⚙️ Procedure:
1. Take 0.5 g finely powdered sample
👉 Slag → crush & powder (200 mesh)
👉 Mill scale → powder
👉 Rust scrap → surface rust scrap karke powder karo

2. Add 50–100 ml dilute H₂SO₄
3. Heat gently (complete dissolution)
4. Filter if residue present
5. Titrate immediately with 0.1 N KMnO₄
6. End point: colourless → light pink (permanent)

🧮 Calculation:
FeO % = (Burette × N × 7.18) / W

For 0.1 N:
FeO % = (Reading × 0.718 × 100) / W

📊 Practical Range:
Slag:
• 18–22% → Best
• >25% → High oxidation

Mill Scale:
• 65–75% → Normal

Rusted Scrap:
• Highly variable (only trend use)

💡 Suggestions:
👉 High FeO → Mn/Si loss ↑
👉 Low FeO → Carbon removal slow
👉 Rust scrap use → heat kWh ↑
👉 Mill scale use → FeO control better

⚠️ Precautions:
👉 KMnO₄ fresh hona chahiye
👉 Titration delay mat karo (Fe²⁺ oxidize ho jayega)
👉 End point overshoot mat karo

🚨 Limitations:
👉 Rust scrap me Fe₂O₃ / Fe₃O₄ bhi hota hai
👉 Result exact FeO nahi hota
👉 Furnace control ke liye trend use karo
Test: SiO₂ – Gravimetric Method (Universal)

🔬 Reagents:
1. Conc. HCl
2. Conc. HNO₃
3. Distilled Water

⚙️ Procedure:
1. Take 0.5 g finely powdered sample
👉 Slag → crush & powder
👉 Ramming mass → fine powder
👉 Sponge iron → fine grinding (important)

2. Add 20–30 ml HCl + few drops HNO₃
👉 Sponge iron case:
Add slightly more HNO₃ for complete dissolution

3. Heat till complete dissolution
4. Evaporate to dryness (very important)
👉 Silica gel formation step (skip mat karo)

5. Add HCl + water, heat again
6. Filter (Whatman 41)
👉 Residue = SiO₂

7. Wash properly (chloride free)
8. Ignite at ~900°C (muffle furnace)
9. Cool & weigh

🧮 Calculation:
SiO₂ % = (Residue Weight / Sample Weight) × 100

📊 Practical Range:
Slag:
• 15–25% → Normal
• High → acidic slag

Ramming Mass:
• 98–99% → Good quality
• <97% → impurity high

Sponge Iron:
• 4–8% → Normal
• >10% → poor quality / gangue high

💡 Suggestions:
👉 Slag:
• High SiO₂ → Lime increase karo (basicity ↑)
• Low SiO₂ → Slag too basic (fluidity issue)

👉 Ramming Mass:
• High SiO₂ → lining life better
• Impurity ↑ → erosion risk

👉 Sponge Iron:
• High SiO₂ → slag volume ↑
• Power consumption ↑
• Mn recovery ↓
• More flux (lime) required

⚠️ Precautions:
👉 Drying step compulsory (gel formation)
👉 Sponge iron properly dissolve karo
👉 Washing complete karo (Cl⁻ remove)
👉 Filter paper ash-free use karo
👉 Accurate weighing जरूरी
Test: CaO – EDTA Titration (Murexide Method)

🔬 Reagents:
1. 0.01 M EDTA Solution

2. Ammonia Buffer (pH ~12)
NH₄Cl + NH₄OH solution

3. Murexide Indicator
(Pink to purple colour change)

4. NaOH Solution
(for high pH adjustment)

⚙️ Procedure:
1. Take solution (from sample digestion)
👉 Slag / RM / Sponge – acid digested solution use karo

2. Add NaOH → pH ~12 maintain karo

3. Add small amount Murexide indicator
👉 Colour: pink

4. Titrate with EDTA (0.01 M)

5. End point:
👉 Pink → Purple / Blue

🧮 Calculation:
CaO % = (V × N × 56 × 100) / W

For 0.01 M EDTA:
CaO % = (V × 0.028 × 100) / W

📊 Practical Range:
Slag:
• 25–35% → Normal
• >40% → Over basic
• <20% → Acidic slag

Ramming Mass:
• Very low expected (<1%)

Sponge Iron:
• 1–5% typical

💡 Suggestions:
👉 Slag:
• Low CaO → Lime add karo
• High CaO → Slag thick ho sakta hai
• Maintain CaO/SiO₂ = 1.2–1.8

👉 Sponge:
• High CaO → flux load ↑

👉 RM:
• High CaO → lining quality poor

⚠️ Precautions:
👉 pH ~12 maintain करना जरूरी है
👉 Indicator overdose मत करो
👉 EDTA standard hona chahiye
👉 End point sharp observe karo
👉 Mg interference avoid karo (high pH)
Test: MgO – EDTA Titration (EBT Method)

🔬 Reagents:
1. 0.01 M EDTA Solution

2. Ammonia Buffer (pH ~10)
NH₄Cl + NH₄OH solution

3. EBT Indicator (Eriochrome Black T)
Wine red → Blue end point

4. Distilled Water

⚙️ Procedure:
1. Take aliquot from sample solution
👉 Same digestion solution (slag / RM / sponge)

2. Add ammonia buffer → pH ~10 maintain karo

3. Add 2–3 drops EBT indicator
👉 Colour: wine red

4. Titrate with 0.01 M EDTA

5. End point:
👉 Wine red → Blue

🧮 Calculation:
MgO % = (V × N × 40 × 100) / W

For 0.01 M EDTA:
MgO % = (V × 0.020 × 100) / W

📊 Practical Range:
Slag:
• 5–10% → Normal
• <4% → Lining attack risk
• >12% → Slag thick

Ramming Mass:
• Very low expected

Sponge Iron:
• 1–3% typical

💡 Suggestions:
👉 Slag:
• Low MgO → dolomite / Mg source add karo
• High MgO → slag fluidity kam ho jayegi
• Maintain balance with CaO

👉 Lining Protection:
• MgO proper ho → silica lining safe
• Low MgO → erosion fast

👉 Furnace Operation:
• Balanced MgO → smooth tapping
• High MgO → slag jam / nozzle issue

⚠️ Precautions:
👉 pH ~10 maintain karo (critical)
👉 EBT fresh hona chahiye
👉 EDTA standard hona chahiye
👉 End point clearly observe karo
👉 Ca interference avoid karo (separate CaO test required)
🧮 Basicity:
Basicity (B) = CaO / SiO₂

📊 Example:
CaO = 30%, SiO₂ = 20%
👉 B = 1.50

📈 Basicity Range:
🔴 B < 1.2 → Acidic Slag
👉 Mn recovery ↓
👉 FeO ↑ (oxidation high)

🟢 B = 1.2 – 1.8 → Ideal Zone
👉 Best Mn recovery
👉 Smooth casting
👉 Stable slag

🔴 B > 1.8 → Over Basic
👉 Slag thick
👉 Flow problem
👉 Nozzle choking risk

⚖️ MgO Role:
• 5–10% → Ideal
• <4% → Lining damage risk
• >12% → Slag thick

🔥 FeO Impact:
• 18–25% → Safe
• >28% → Mn loss high
• <15% → Carbon removal slow

🧠 Full Slag Control:
👉 Balance of:
• CaO (basicity)
• SiO₂ (acidic)
• MgO (stability)
• FeO (oxidation)

💡 Practical Actions:
👉 Low Basicity (B <1.2):
• Lime add karo
• Silica load kam karo

👉 High Basicity (B >1.8):
• Lime kam karo
• Silica sand add karo

👉 Low MgO:
• Dolomite add karo

👉 High FeO:
• Carbon / CI add karo
• Alloy delay karo

⚠️ Common Mistakes:
❌ Sirf CaO dekhna
❌ MgO ignore karna
❌ High FeO me alloy add karna
❌ Over liming karna

🚀 Plant Benefit:
✔ Mn recovery ↑
✔ Power saving
✔ Smooth casting
✔ Lining life ↑
Test: Water Hardness (as CaCO₃)

🔬 Reagents:
1. 0.01 M EDTA Solution
2. Ammonia Buffer (pH ~10)
3. EBT Indicator (Eriochrome Black T)

⚙️ Procedure:
1. Take 50 ml water sample

2. Add 5 ml buffer (pH ~10)

3. Add 2–3 drops EBT indicator
👉 Colour: wine red

4. Titrate with EDTA

5. End point:
👉 Wine red → Blue

🧮 Calculation:
Hardness (ppm) = (V × 1000) / Sample (ml)

👉 For 50 ml sample:
Hardness = V × 20

📊 Standard Range & Use:
🥤 Drinking Water:
• 0–100 ppm → Best
• 100–200 ppm → Acceptable
• >300 ppm → Not good

🔥 Furnace Cooling Water:
• <100 ppm → Good
• <50 ppm → Best

🏭 CCM Cooling Water:
• <50 ppm → Ideal
• 50–100 ppm → Acceptable
• >100 ppm → Scaling risk

💡 Practical Suggestions:
👉 High Hardness (>100 ppm):
• Scale formation ↑
• Nozzle choking risk
• Heat transfer ↓
• Cooling efficiency ↓

👉 Low Hardness (<50 ppm):
• Best for CCM
• No scaling
• Smooth casting

👉 Control Methods:
• Softener use karo
• RO system use karo

⚠️ Important Note:
👉 CCM water ka TDS ideally:
100–150 ppm ke niche hona chahiye

👉 Hardness aur TDS dono control me hona जरूरी है
